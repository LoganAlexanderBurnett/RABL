\section{Reactor Plant Physics and Modeling Details}
\label{ap:plantmodel}

This appendix specifies the reduced heat-pipe microreactor and balance-of-plant model used to generate the transient response trajectories. The Modelica implementation, \texttt{MicroreactorPK.Models.HPMicroPK}, is simulated through \texttt{MicroreactorPK.Experiments.RunOneProfile}. It couples six-group point kinetics to five dynamic thermal nodes representing fuel, moderator/graphite, an effective heat-pipe node, the nitrogen loop, and active steam-generator heat-transfer metal. The secondary water/steam inventory is not an additional dynamic thermal node. Instead, the heat transferred from the steam-generator metal determines algebraic steam outlet temperature and quality. A single prescribed angle is applied to twelve control drums.

\subsection{Neutronics and drum reactivity}

The normalized neutron population $n$ defines reactor thermal power $P=P_r n$, with $P_r=6$~MW. The six delayed-neutron precursor variables $c_i$ and neutron population obey
\begin{align}
 \frac{dn}{dt}
 &=\frac{\rho-\beta}{\Lambda}n+\sum_{i=1}^{6}\lambda_i c_i,
 \label{eq:app:neutron}\\
 \frac{dc_i}{dt}
 &=\frac{\beta_i}{\Lambda}n-\lambda_i c_i,\qquad i=1,\ldots,6,
 \label{eq:app:precursor}
\end{align}
where $\beta=\sum_i\beta_i$, $\lambda_i$ are group decay constants, and $\Lambda$ is the prompt neutron generation time. The control-drum contribution is referenced to the initial angle $\theta_0=45^\circ$:
\begin{align}
 \rho_d(\theta)
 &=n_d\rho_{\max}^{(1)}
  \frac{1-\cos(\pi\theta/180)}{2}-\rho_{\mathrm{ss,tot}},
 \label{eq:app:drum}\\
 \rho_{\mathrm{ss,tot}}
 &=n_d\rho_{\max}^{(1)}
  \frac{1-\cos(\pi\theta_0/180)}{2}.
\end{align}
Here $\rho_{\max}^{(1)}$ is the worth assigned to one drum and $n_d=12$. Total reactivity includes the fuel and moderator feedback terms,
\begin{equation}
 \rho(t)=\rho_d(\theta(t))
    +\alpha_f[T_f(t)-T_{f,0}]
    +\alpha_m[T_m(t)-T_{m,0}],
 \qquad \rho_{\$}(t)=\frac{\rho(t)}{\beta}.
 \label{eq:app:reactivity}
\end{equation}
Reactivity $\rho$ is in $\Delta k/k$ and $\rho_{\$}$ is in dollars. The angle is an externally specified continuous-time input; the input block linearly interpolates its tabulated values and holds the final value beyond the supplied profile. Drum velocity and acceleration may be saved with the profile but do not introduce additional dynamical states in this model.

\subsection{Five-node thermal network}

Let $T_f$, $T_m$, $T_{hp}$, $T_{N_2}$, and $T_{sg}$ denote the five thermal states. The first four represent the fuel, moderator/graphite, heat-pipe equivalent, and average nitrogen-loop temperatures. The fifth, $T_{sg}$, represents the active heat-transfer tube metal of the steam generator; its capacitance excludes the water/steam inventory, headers, and other unmodeled structure. With positive effective thermal conductances $G$, the implemented energy balances can be written
\begin{align}
 M_fc_{p,f}\frac{dT_f}{dt}
 &=f_hP+G_{f,g}(T_m-T_f),
 \label{eq:app:fuel}\\
 M_gc_{p,g}\frac{dT_m}{dt}
 &=(1-f_h)P+G_{f,g}(T_f-T_m)
     +G_{g,hp}(T_{hp}-T_m),
 \label{eq:app:moderator}\\
 M_{hp}c_{p,hp}\frac{dT_{hp}}{dt}
 &=G_{g,hp}(T_m-T_{hp})
     +G_{hp,N_2}(T_{N_2}-T_{hp}),
 \label{eq:app:heatpipe}\\
 M_{N_2}c_{p,N_2}\frac{dT_{N_2}}{dt}
 &=G_{hp,N_2}(T_{hp}-T_{N_2})
     -G_{N_2,sg}(T_{N_2}-T_{sg}),
 \label{eq:app:nitrogen}\\
 C_{sg}\frac{dT_{sg}}{dt}
 &=G_{N_2,sg}(T_{N_2}-T_{sg})-Q_{sg,fw}.
 \label{eq:app:steammetal}
\end{align}
The deposited power fraction is $f_h=0.90$ in the fuel; the remainder enters the moderator/structure lump. The nitrogen-node effective mass is obtained using an assumed residence time, $M_{N_2}=\dot m_{N_2}\tau_N$. The conductances are based on rated power and nominal inter-node temperature differences, as detailed in Table~\ref{tab:plant-thermal}. Equivalently, the original Modelica equations use direction-specific time constants $\tau_{a\to b}=C_a/G_{a,b}$ for the first four thermal nodes. Equations~\eqref{eq:app:fuel}--\eqref{eq:app:steammetal} express the same energy balances directly.

\subsection{Steam-generator heat transfer and outlet state}

The secondary stream is modeled at fixed feedwater mass flow $\dot m_{fw}$ and specified outlet pressure $p_{\mathrm{out}}$. Its pressure is not a dynamic variable; it is used to set prescribed saturation properties. At the chosen operating point, the feedwater inlet temperature is taken equal to the specified saturation temperature, $T_{fw,\mathrm{in}}=T_{\mathrm{sat}}=471.45$~K. The heat received by the active steam-generator metal and the nonnegative heat transferred to the secondary stream are
\begin{align}
 Q_{N_2,sg}
 &=G_{N_2,sg}(T_{N_2}-T_{sg}),
 \label{eq:app:sgnitrogen}\\
 Q_{sg,fw}
 &=\max\!\left[G_{sg,fw}(T_{sg}-T_{\mathrm{sat}}),\,0\right].
 \label{eq:app:sgfeedwater}
\end{align}
The nonnegative constraint prevents reverse secondary heat addition in this simplified model. The specific enthalpy added to the fixed feedwater stream is $q_{sg}=Q_{sg,fw}/\dot m_{fw}$. Using the prescribed latent heat $h_{fg}$ and effective superheated-steam heat capacity $c_{p,\mathrm{sh}}$, the outlet dryness fraction and temperature are
\begin{align}
 x_{\mathrm{steam,out}}
 &=\min\!\left(1,\max\!\left[0,\frac{q_{sg}}{h_{fg}}\right]\right),
 \label{eq:app:steamquality}\\
 T_{\mathrm{steam,out}}
 &=T_{\mathrm{sat}}
    +\frac{\max(q_{sg}-h_{fg},0)}{c_{p,\mathrm{sh}}}.
 \label{eq:app:steamtemperature}
\end{align}
Thus, when the heat addition is insufficient to vaporize the entire flow, the outlet temperature remains at saturation and the quality varies below unity. Once the flow reaches unit quality, additional heat raises the outlet temperature above saturation. This closure does not resolve two-phase fluid inventory, pressure losses, or a dynamic steam/water energy balance. In particular, $T_{sg}$ is an independently evolving \emph{metal} temperature, whereas $T_{\mathrm{steam,out}}$ is a calculated fluid outlet condition.

\subsection{Initial conditions and predicted quantities}

The differential system has twelve states: normalized power, six precursor quantities, and five temperatures. The specified initial conditions are
\begin{align}
 n(0)&=1,&
 c_i(0)&=\frac{\beta_i}{\Lambda\lambda_i},&
 T_f(0)&=T_{f,0},&
 T_m(0)&=T_{m,0},\nonumber\\
 T_{hp}(0)&=T_{hp,0},&
 T_{N_2}(0)&=T_{N_2,0},&
 T_{sg}(0)&=T_{sg,0}.&
 \label{eq:app:initial}
\end{align}
The remaining three surrogate output variables, reactivity in dollars, steam outlet temperature, and steam outlet quality, follow algebraically from the states and control input. Accordingly, the neural surrogate predicts fifteen output variables: the twelve dynamic states and these three algebraic responses. Physical power in MW, $P_{\mathrm{MW}}=P/10^6$, is also available for visualization in place of the normalized neutron-population output $n$. The experiment wrapper specifies a 200~s simulation and Dymola solver tolerance of $10^{-8}$.

The conductances are calculated from the nominal rated-power differences:
\begin{align}
 G_{f,g}&=\frac{f_hP_r}{|T_{f,0}-T_{m,0}|},
 &G_{g,hp}&=\frac{P_r}{|T_{m,0}-T_{hp,0}|},\nonumber\\
 G_{hp,N_2}&=\frac{P_r}{|T_{hp,0}-T_{N_2,0}|},
 &G_{N_2,sg}&=\frac{P_r}{|T_{N_2,0}-T_{sg,0}|},\nonumber\\
 G_{sg,fw}&=\frac{P_r}{|T_{sg,0}-T_{\mathrm{sat}}|},
 &\dot m_{N_2}&=\frac{P_r}{c_{p,N_2}(T_{N_2,\mathrm{out}}-T_{N_2,\mathrm{in}})}.
 \label{eq:app:conductances}
\end{align}
The effective superheated-steam specific heat is calculated from the nominal superheat rise, $c_{p,\mathrm{sh}}=h_{\mathrm{sh,nom}}/(T_{\mathrm{steam,nom}}-T_{\mathrm{sat}})$. For the active SG metal, $\tau_{sg\to N_2}=C_{sg}/G_{N_2,sg}$ and $\tau_{sg\to fw}=C_{sg}/G_{sg,fw}$ are individual-side diagnostic time constants; the combined small-signal value is $C_{sg}/(G_{N_2,sg}+G_{sg,fw})$. These quantities represent the simplified model's selected lumped dynamics and are not independently validated component measurements.
