\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This study investigated the connected requirements of temporally structured simulation data, accurate recursive forecasting, and calibrated uncertainty for multivariate transients driven by prescribed control trajectories. The proposed Multivariate Adaptive Conformal Ensemble (MACE) framework integrates stochastic control velocities to generate continuous control trajectories, uses a ten-member LSTM ensemble whose members advance independently through their own predicted histories, and constructs conformal intervals with forecast-dependent scales derived from ensemble disagreement. Separate calibrations were considered for individual forecast horizons, complete trajectories of each output variable, and complete multivariate response trajectories. The framework was evaluated using a reduced heat-pipe microreactor and balance-of-plant simulator with 15 output variables over a 200~s forecast horizon.

The ensemble accurately reproduced complete autoregressive forecast trajectories in the independently generated evaluation populations. Pooled mean absolute error and root mean square error, normalized by the original training-set output ranges, were 0.00154 and 0.00359 on the stochastic test set and 0.00141 and 0.00256 on the operation-informed test set, respectively. Under matched stochastic calibration and evaluation, MACE-Horizon reduced mean normalized full interval width by 8.9\% and mean interval score by 34.6\% relative to Absolute CP-Horizon, while MACE-Trajectory reduced the corresponding metrics by 43.9\% and 50.0\% relative to Absolute CP-Trajectory. Both comparisons maintained empirical coverage near the nominal 95\% level for their respective events, and the reductions in width and interval score were observed for all 15 output variables. Joint complete-multivariate trajectory calibration further yielded coverage of 0.9525 with mean normalized width of 0.0169 for MACE, compared with coverage of 0.9445 and width of 0.0398 for joint absolute-error calibration. These findings establish an interval-efficiency advantage for ensemble-adaptive scaling under the evaluated matched-distribution conditions.

The operation-informed experiments demonstrate that this advantage does not by itself ensure reliable coverage under a change in the control-trajectory distribution. When thresholds obtained from stochastic calibration trajectories were transferred to the same independently generated operation-informed evaluation forecasts, pooled per-output complete-trajectory coverage for MACE-Trajectory was 0.6800. Replacing those thresholds with quantiles calibrated on an independent operation-informed sample increased coverage to 0.9549, a paired difference of 0.2749 with a descriptive 95\% whole-trajectory bootstrap interval of [0.2666, 0.2831]. Recalibration left the ensemble predictions unchanged and did not improve all interval metrics simultaneously. For MACE-Horizon, pooled coverage increased from 0.9479 to 0.9559 while normalized mean width decreased from 0.0073 to 0.0062. For MACE-Trajectory, recovering the more demanding coverage objective required an increase in mean width from 0.0121 to 0.0136 and in mean interval score from 0.0123 to 0.0137. After operation-informed recalibration, MACE nevertheless retained lower pooled widths than the corresponding absolute-error methods for both Horizon and per-output Trajectory coverage.

The observed association between ensemble spread and absolute forecast error supports using disagreement to adapt interval width to forecast-dependent variations in predictive difficulty. The conformal calibration distribution, however, remains essential to the statistical interpretation of those intervals. Standard split-conformal coverage relies on exchangeable calibration and evaluation trajectory samples and does not guarantee coverage for individual maneuver types or populations with different temporal characteristics. Moreover, the present evaluation uses a single reduced simulator, prescribed control trajectories, a fixed forecast horizon, and fixed trained and calibrated models; its results do not constitute validation on an operating reactor. Broader control regimes, higher-fidelity physical models, repeated training and calibration samples, and methods for recalibration under changing conditions are therefore appropriate directions for further investigation. The principal conclusion is that ensemble disagreement can improve the efficiency of conformal prediction for control-conditioned multivariate forecasts, provided that calibration reflects the trajectories on which the intervals are to be evaluated.
