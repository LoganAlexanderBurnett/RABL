Forecasting coupled multivariate transients under prescribed control trajectories requires temporally structured simulation data, accurate recursive prediction, and prediction intervals with explicit coverage objectives. This study develops the Multivariate Adaptive Conformal Ensemble (MACE) framework, which combines stochastic control trajectories obtained by integrating temporally correlated velocities with a ten-member autoregressive long short-term memory ensemble. Each member advances using its own predicted histories. Split conformal prediction uses ensemble spread with an additive floor as a forecast-dependent interval scale, targeting horizon-specific, per-output complete-trajectory, and joint complete-multivariate trajectory coverage. Corresponding absolute-error conformal methods provide baselines. The framework is evaluated using a reduced heat-pipe microreactor and balance-of-plant simulator, forecasting 15 output variables over 200~s. Pooled normalized mean absolute errors were 0.00154 on stochastic trajectories and 0.00141 on independent operation-informed trajectories. Under matched stochastic calibration, MACE reduced mean full interval width by 8.9\% for Horizon and 43.9\% for per-output Trajectory calibration relative to the corresponding absolute methods, with approximately nominal 95\% empirical coverage. Mean interval scores decreased by 34.6\% and 50.0\%, respectively. Transferring stochastic calibration thresholds to operation-informed forecasts yielded mean per-output complete-trajectory coverage of 0.6800 for MACE-Trajectory, increasing to 0.9549 following independent operation-informed recalibration of the same forecasts. These results demonstrate the usefulness of ensemble disagreement as an adaptive interval scale while emphasizing the dependence of conformal coverage on an appropriate calibration distribution.
